The Science of Reaction Time

Human reaction time is defined as the elapsed duration between the onset of a sensory stimulus and the physical motor response. When the ReflexRush game screen switches from red to green, an intricate biological sequence unfolds in a fraction of a second:

  1. Sensory Reception: Photoreceptor cells in your retina detect the shift in wavelength to green light and generate electrochemical impulses.
  2. Transmission: The optic nerve carries these signals through the thalamus directly to the primary visual cortex in the occipital lobe.
  3. Decision & Motor Command: The prefrontal and motor cortices process the visual cue, recognizing it as the trigger signal, and dispatch motor commands down the spinal cord.
  4. Muscle Contraction: Motor neurons stimulate muscle fibers in your forearm and index finger, pressing down on the touchscreen or keyboard.

In healthy young adults, visual reaction times typically fall between 200 and 270 milliseconds. Competitive esports players and professional athletes frequently reach times between 170 and 210 milliseconds.

Hardware & Latency Factors

When you play ReflexRush, your measured score accounts for both your biological reflex and subtle physical hardware delays:

  • Display Refresh Rate: A standard 60Hz monitor refreshes every 16.6ms, while a 144Hz gaming display refreshes every 6.9ms, delivering the green visual trigger faster.
  • Input Polling: Modern touchscreen digitizers and high-polling USB gaming mice register touch contacts with minimal latency, giving you an authentic millisecond benchmark.

Designed for Fair Competition

Unlike basic single-click tests that can be distorted by lucky guesses or anticipation, ReflexRush calculates a 5-round average while dynamically randomizing delay intervals between 1.5 and 4.5 seconds. Premature clicks are immediately flagged with a penalty alert, ensuring that every score represents genuine human reaction.
