The Neuroscience of the Stroop Effect

Discovered in 1935 by psychologist John Ridley Stroop, this is one of the most famous tests in experimental psychology.

1

Automatic Word Reading

Reading words is an automated subconscious process for literate humans. When you see the letters B-L-U-E, your brain decodes the meaning before you can even register what color the ink is.

2

Anterior Cingulate Cortex

Resolving the conflict between the word meaning and the font color activates your Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC) and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex to suppress the incorrect automatic impulse.

3

Cognitive Flexibility

High scores indicate high cognitive flexibility, fast selective attention, and superior working-memory filtering under time pressure.
