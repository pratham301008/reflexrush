The Science of Working Memory

Why do most people struggle past 7 digits, and how can you train yourself to beat 12?

1

Miller's Magical Number 7

In 1956, Harvard cognitive psychologist George A. Miller published The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two. It established that human working memory capacity is strictly limited to 5 to 9 "chunks" of information.

2

The "Chunking" Secret

Grandmasters don't memorize 12 individual numbers; they group them into chunks of 3 or 4. For instance, instead of `8 - 3 - 9 - 1 - 4 - 2`, think `839` and `142`. This turns 6 pieces of data into only 2 chunks!

3

Auditory Echo Loop

Whispering or mentally pronouncing the rhythm of the digits uses the brain's phonological loop, holding information in acoustic memory for an extra 1.5 to 2 seconds before you type.
